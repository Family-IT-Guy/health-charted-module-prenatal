## Tier 1: Broadly Safe to Supplement Without Testing

Strong evidence, wide safety margins, widespread dietary insufficiency:

- **Methylfolate** 800-1000 mcg/day
- **Vitamin D3** 2000-4000 IU/day (adjust based on serum 25(OH)D)
- **DHA** 300+ mg/day
- **Choline** 450-550 mg/day

## Tier 2: Supplement Based on Measurement

Individual variance is high. Supplementing without testing can be unnecessary or counterproductive:

- **Iron**: only if ferritin <50 mcg/L. Unnecessary iron causes oxidative stress (Fenton chemistry), GI symptoms, and can inhibit zinc absorption. HFE carriers (hemochromatosis genes) should avoid routine iron entirely.
- **B12**: critical for vegans/vegetarians; measure first for omnivores.
- **Iodine**: depends on dietary iodized salt and dairy consumption. Narrowest safety margin of any pregnancy nutrient.
- **Calcium**: depends on dietary intake. Target ~1000 mg/day total (diet + supplements).

## Tier 3: Approach With Serious Care

Narrow therapeutic windows. Can cause harm if dosed wrong:

- **Vitamin A as retinol**: TERATOGENIC above ~10,000 IU. Excess retinol overwhelms CYP26A1 degradation, causing supraphysiological retinoic acid that disrupts neural crest migration and HOX gene expression. Beta-carotene is safe (self-limiting conversion). Always check the form in any supplement or skincare product. See [[vitamin-a-teratogenicity-mechanism]].
- **High-dose iron without confirmed deficiency**: oxidative stress via Fenton chemistry. Weekly dosing avoids this if supplementing prophylactically. See [[iron-oxidative-stress-mechanism]].
- **High-dose iodine**: can cause thyroid dysfunction. Stay within 150-250 mcg/day supplemental.
- **B6 at high doses**: UL is 100 mg/day. Chronic high-dose causes peripheral neuropathy.

## Upper Tolerable Intake Levels (ULs) During Pregnancy

| Nutrient | UL | Notes |
|----------|-----|-------|
| Vitamin A (retinol) | 3,000 mcg RAE (10,000 IU) | Teratogenic above this. Beta-carotene exempt. |
| Vitamin D | 4,000 IU (conservative) | Toxicity onset much higher (~40,000 IU chronic). 6,400 IU used safely in lactation RCTs. |
| Iron | No formal UL | Hepcidin self-regulates. GI effects and oxidative stress at high doses without deficiency. |
| Iodine | 1,100 mcg | Narrowest margin of any pregnancy nutrient |
| Zinc | 40 mg | |
| Calcium | 2,500 mg | |
| Folate | 1,000 mcg (synthetic folic acid only) | Methylfolate does not have this concern |
| B6 | 100 mg | Neuropathy risk above this |
| B12 | No UL | Excess excreted |
| Selenium | 400 mcg | |
| Magnesium | 350 mg (supplemental only) | Dietary magnesium has no UL |
| Vitamin E | 1,000 mg | Bleeding risk at high doses |
| Vitamin C | 2,000 mg | |
