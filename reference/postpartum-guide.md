## Breast Milk Nutrient Categories

See [[lactation:group-i-vs-group-ii-breast-milk-nutrients]] for the full framework.

### Group I: Diet-dependent (mother's status directly affects infant intake)

Vitamin D, DHA/omega-3, iodine, selenium, B vitamins (B1, B2, B6, B12), choline, vitamins A, E, K.

Supplementing these nutrients directly improves what the infant receives through breast milk.

### Group II: Diet-independent (body maintains milk content at mother's expense)

Calcium, iron, zinc, folate, copper.

The infant gets what it needs regardless. The mother's body is depleted to maintain milk concentration. Replenishment is required to avoid severe maternal depletion. This is why postpartum depletion can be severe even when the infant appears well-nourished.

## Lactation-Specific Supplementation

| Nutrient | Dose | Evidence | Rationale |
|----------|------|----------|-----------|
| Vitamin D | 6,400 IU/day | RCT (Hollis et al., n=334, 2015) | Increases breast-milk vitamin D ~10-fold. Infant status equivalent to direct infant 400 IU supplementation. Standard prenatal dose (400-2000 IU) does NOT produce adequate milk levels. See [[lactation:lactation-vitamin-d-evidence]]. |
| DHA | 300-400 mg/day | Moderate | 400 mg increases milk DHA by ~123%. Breast-milk DHA directly depends on maternal intake. |
| Iron | 80-100 mg/day if anemic; ~27 mg maintenance | Strong | Replenish stores. Check ferritin after ~6 weeks (first reliable reading post-inflammation). |
| Iodine | 150-250 mcg/day | Moderate-Strong | Most sensitive breast-milk nutrient. Infant entirely dependent on maternal supply. |
| Selenium | 60-75 mcg/day (200 mcg if TPO+) | Moderate | Reduces postpartum thyroiditis from ~48.6% to ~28.6% in TPO+ patients. |
| Zinc | ~12 mg/day | Moderate | ~87.5% postpartum deficiency rate. Group II nutrient depletes mother. |
| EPA | 1-2 g for mood support | Moderate | EPA (not DHA) shows largest effect for postpartum depression. |
| Choline | 450-550 mg/day | Moderate | Group I: varies with maternal intake. |

## Postpartum Mental Health Connections

- Low ferritin at 48 hours post-birth: ~4x postpartum-depression risk
- Vitamin D deficiency: ~2.28x PPD risk
- EPA supplementation: largest mood effect (SDM = -0.886 for PPD)
- Postpartum thyroiditis (~8% prevalence): screen TSH at 3 and 6 months; selenium reduces risk in TPO+ patients; ~30% develop permanent hypothyroidism. See [[postpartum:postpartum-thyroiditis]].
